01 / The opportunity

Learn the constraints before tuning the search.

This report is specific to CP/2155_CP1: both starter notebooks, the supplied LINKS implementation, the three target arrays, the local research library, and primary sources. The recommended changes below are hypotheses to test; no optimizer was run and no submission score or improvement is claimed.

1

Make more useful candidates.

Study dyadic construction and full-cycle closure. Topology that the solver can order still needs geometry that can move.

2

Optimize the actual task.

Understand alignment, scale, material, and hypervolume. A beautiful curve or a single champion is only part of the objective.

3

Spend refinement wisely.

Use existing gradients on several distinct, valid designs. Keep useful parents when a refinement trades away too much material.

Method and limits

A discrete Fourier transform is applied separately to x and y over the 200 points in their stored order. The mean and frequencies ±1 through ±K are retained; no scale or pose fitting is performed. A periodic wrap is assumed for this illustration, even though each array's first and last points differ. The displayed RMS is a reconstruction diagnostic, not CP1's distance. The score uses its own arc-length resampling and correspondence procedure. Harmonic count is not a predicted number of joints. The multiscale-search idea is an inference inspired by Sharma et al. and Qian et al., whose methods concern four-bar synthesis.

Why a linkage locks: two circles must intersect.

Lengths AC = 1.00 and BC = 0.80. At d = 0.20 or 1.80, the two solutions merge. Outside that interval no real joint position satisfies both lengths.

m = min(d − |r₁ − r₂|, r₁ + r₂ − d)

Positive m is a local geometric clearance from tangency, not a global robustness certificate or score. The hollow marker is the other assembly solution. Switching the menu compares separate assemblies; it does not simulate passing through a singularity. Circle geometry is derived here; the solver connection is supported by LinkEdit §3–4 and L2.

03 / Knowledge map

10 topics

Start here · Kinematics

01. Mobility, dyads, and the solver's admissible mechanisms

A connected graph is not necessarily a movable mechanism—or a mechanism this solver can solve.

Domain knowledge. Mobility counts independent motion. For a generic planar rigid-body assembly, Grübler's formula is M = 3(L − 1) − 2J₁ − J₂, with L including ground and J₁/J₂ counting one-/two-DOF joints. Dependent constraints and special geometries can invalidate a simple count. The LINKS graph instead stores joint positions and pairwise distances; its node count is not L. [S2]

Project connection. dyadic_path starts with fixed joints and the motor endpoint. Each unknown must have exactly two already-known neighbors. Fewer known neighbors can halt ordering; more than two is rejected as redundant/overconstrained. This is narrower than all one-DOF planar mechanisms. LinkEdit explains the sequential reconstruction family. [S1]

Use it. Prefer a crank-and-ground seed with successive two-parent joint additions. Reuse MechanismRandomizer before designing a new generator. Preserve distinct parents, valid motor grounding, and a meaningful moving output. An orderable graph still needs a valid complete motion cycle.

Code: _kin.py:4–82; _MechanismRandomizer.py:146 onward.

Start here · Feasibility

02. Full rotation, assembly branches, and singularities

Initial closure is easy. Maintaining it over the entire input cycle is the real constraint.

Domain knowledge. A four-bar's Grashof relation s + l ≤ p + q concerns relative rotation; the grounded link and chosen input determine whether that input can revolve. Equality is a change-point case. Do not apply the four-bar test as a certificate for a large linkage. Transmission quality also depends on angle: nearly collinear links can transmit torque poorly. [S3]

Project connection. LINKS computes an arccos from the triangle lengths and preserves a signed orientation from x0. A triangle becoming flat creates a singularity and merges the two assembly solutions. The derivative d(arccos c)/dc = −1/√(1 − c²) explains why gradients become ill-conditioned near |c| = 1. A non-finite gradient and an impossible configuration are different diagnoses. [S1] [L2]

Use it. Inspect the minimum circle-closure margin across all reconstructed joints and input angles as a diagnostic. Try shorter local-search steps near degeneracy, preserve the last valid parent, and inspect denser-angle motion separately from the official 200-sample evaluation. Dense sampling increases confidence but is not a proof of continuous feasibility.

Do not “repair” a truly impossible triangle by clipping every arccos argument into [−1, 1]. Clipping may address tiny roundoff; it can also conceal real loss of closure. Any evaluator change would need a separate diagnosis.

Code: _JAX.py:20–43; _DifferentiableTools.py.

Start here · Objective geometry

03. Path generation, correspondence, and scale

Know which differences the score removes and which differences it still penalizes.

Domain knowledge. Path generation specifies the locus of a point; prescribed timing additionally ties positions to input angles. Fourier descriptors depend on parameterization. Sharma et al. show that uniform parameter assignments can restrict four-bar fitting; Qian et al. develop a timing-assignment method that excludes order-defective combinations. Neither is a drop-in CP1 objective. [S5] [S6]

What this implementation does. It resamples the target and generated polyline by arc length, centers them separately, and divides both by the target's RMS radius. It tries cyclic index shifts and reversed traversal. For each ordering it fits a rotation analytically, then compares the mean Euclidean pointwise distances multiplied by 2π. This is an ordered comparison, despite “Chamfer” wording in one notebook comment. Reversing traversal does not mirror the shape.

D = minₖ (2π / n) Σᵢ ‖tᵢ − Rₖ cπₖ(i)‖₂

Here t and c are the centered, target-radius-normalized samples; πₖ is a cyclic/reversed ordering. Rₖ is the code's fitted rotation (from dot/cross sums), not a separate numerical minimization of the unsquared norm above.

Use it. Never independently normalize candidate size before claiming better official fitness. A smaller lookalike can have worse D. Treat descriptors as screening or initialization aids, and return to Tools for selection. Preserve explicit target_joint when reordering and saving mechanisms.

Code: Geometry/_utils.py:4–90; Optimization/_utils.py:10–53; _Tools.py:214–225.

Start here · Multiobjective design

04. Pareto fronts and the value of a collection

Curve accuracy, material, and archive quality are related—but not interchangeable.

Domain knowledge. A design dominates another when it is no worse in both objectives and strictly better in at least one. NSGA-II combines nondominated sorting with crowding distance to retain a spread of designs. SMS-EMOA instead uses hypervolume contribution to guide survival. The latter is conceptually close to the challenge's set-level objective. [S7] [S8]

Project connection. Only candidates strictly inside a target's reference box contribute. Duplicates and dominated points add no new dominated area. Improving a low-error design can be less useful than discovering an inexpensive design in a sparse region. Replacing a parent with a more accurate but materially heavier child can reduce collection quality.

Use it. Maintain the union of valid parents and children, remove dominated points, and retain distinct tradeoffs. If the archive exceeds 1,000 entries, compare contribution-based pruning using existing pymoo tools against current selection. Preserve the evaluator's reference points and normalization. A nondominated archive can still contain several geometrically different designs with similar scores; that diversity may help later search even when it adds little immediate hypervolume.

Research inference. Track improvement in normalized score per unit of search time for each target. This can guide extra budget after a baseline exists for all three, without assuming that all targets respond equally to more computation.

Code: CP/__init__.py:73–156; the notebooks already use NSGA2 and pymoo.indicators.hv.HV.

Start here · Physical meaning

05. Material is a geometric surrogate, with a local convention

Read the implementation before interpreting “less material” as a physical engineering claim.

Code-derived finding. The solver makes a symmetric adjacency matrix; the material function sums edge lengths over both matrix axes. Therefore, for ordinary undirected edges, the returned material is twice the sum of unique edge lengths. It does not divide by two. This is the local scoring convention and must remain intact when evaluating submissions. [L3]

Mlocal = ΣᵢΣⱼ Aᵢⱼ ‖xᵢ − xⱼ‖₂ = 2 Σunique edges ℓₑ

Implication. Under uniform positive scaling x ↦ αx, this material surrogate scales by α. Distance generally changes because the target scale stays fixed. Thus size is a genuine design variable, not a nuisance normalization to erase. Joint count is a cap, not a term in M.

What it does not establish. This score does not directly model member thickness, density, stiffness, buckling, motor torque, bearing cost, or collision clearance. ReLink is useful background for discrete stock parts and environmental tradeoffs, but its environmental objective is different from CP1's length surrogate. [S11]

Use it. Learn to separate the contest objective from physical cost. Test small uniform rescalings of valid designs and re-evaluate both objectives, retaining useful alternatives. Do not halve the score to make it resemble physical length.

Code: _Solver.py:169–170; Optimization/_utils.py:96–101.

Test next · Representation

06. Mechanism grammars and topology-preserving variation

Search becomes more productive when its representation reflects the allowed mechanism family.

Domain knowledge. Topology, dimensions, and motion are coupled, but topology is discrete. Pan et al. formulate their joint search as mixed-integer convex programming. Their sequential mechanism representation is relevant; the global-optimality claim applies to their approximate formulation, not every physical linkage or this scorer. They report scaling and geometric-relaxation limitations. [S4]

Project connection. At N = 20 the advanced notebook's adjacency encoding exposes 189 independent edge bits after fixing the motor edge, plus joint-type bits, positions, and output choice. Most arbitrary bit patterns have no reason to satisfy the ordering rules. The supplied randomizer already adds joints from earlier parent pairs.

Proposed experiment. Encode each moving joint by two distinct earlier parents. Mutate one parent choice or one local geometric block while preserving the motor and grounding rules. Compare valid-offspring fraction and official final score at matched budgets. Parent order alone does not guarantee circle closure, and restricting a grammar may exclude useful topologies.

Diversity caution. Coordinate distances are meaningful only for compatible topology and consistent labels. Start with topology buckets and within-bucket geometric comparison; do not cluster arbitrary flattened arrays from different mechanisms. Output-joint identity must survive every remapping.

Code: advanced notebook, mechanism_synthesis_optimization / convert_1D_to_mech; _MechanismRandomizer.py.

Test next · Local search

07. Sensitivities, constrained refinement, and numerical validity

The gradients already exist. The useful question is how to spend them without losing good designs.

Domain knowledge. Local sensitivity links small design changes to output changes. Gonzalez Rojas et al. use natural-coordinate constraints and a Lagrangian steepest-descent scheme on fixed topologies, including Jansen mechanisms. This supports studying constraint-aware dimensional optimization; it does not solve topology discovery. [S10]

Project connection. DifferentiableTools returns distance, material, and both gradients with respect to x0. Discrete connectivity, fixed-joint choices, and output identity are not differentiated. Correspondence switches and near-singular triangles also limit smoothness. JAX documents that invalid intermediate operations can contaminate reverse-mode gradients despite a masked finite output. [S13]

Proposed experiment. Minimize D with M ≤ B for several material budgets B below the reference bound. A bounded step with rejection is a simple start; existing SciPy SLSQP supports constraints and supplied derivatives if a stronger local method is justified. Keep finite, valid parents; verify every accepted child with Tools. Then merge children into the archive, rather than overwrite it. [S14]

Source-inspection concern, not an executed diagnosis: Optimization/_utils.py:51 and :92 mask results using has_nans * jnp.inf. Zero times infinity is NaN in ordinary floating-point arithmetic. Confirm finite outputs for known-valid mechanisms in the actual compiled environment before interpreting optimizer results. No scorer change or substitute score was made for this report.

Code: _DifferentiableTools.py; Optimization/_utils.py:51,92,103–105.

Test next · Exploration

08. Multistart search and choosing which candidates to refine

Ten nearly identical local starts can cost ten times as much without finding ten different solutions.

Evidence. Sancibrian et al. compare differential evolution with elite-selected and cluster-selected local-search hybrids. In their fixed-topology, single-error examples, hybrids improved accuracy; plain DE reached convergence faster. The paper supports testing candidate selection, not assuming that a hybrid will improve CP1 hypervolume or runtime. [S9]

Proposed adaptation. Select a few representatives across topology buckets and across distance–material tradeoffs. Within a shared, consistently indexed topology, normalized pairwise geometry can help avoid duplicate starts. An inexpensive farthest-point choice from the current population is worth trying before adding a clustering system. There is no need for external training data.

Comparator. Differential evolution is a useful non-ML baseline for bounded continuous dimensions. SciPy provides it already. A single scalar DE run is not a replacement for a two-objective archive; compare multiple material budgets or use it only within a defined refinement experiment. Check available options against the project's actual installed SciPy version. [S15]

Decision rule. Compare final normalized hypervolume, validity rate, and warmed runtime across the same seeds and target set. Keep global-search and local-search evaluation counts separate so that additional refinement effort is visible.

Test next · Shape analysis

09. Harmonics, feature scale, and continuation

Separate fitting the body from resolving small ears, turns, and the tail.

Domain knowledge. A periodic contour can be decomposed into low-frequency structure and higher-frequency detail. Fourier methods offer a compact representation, but sampling and timing choices matter. The cited four-bar work makes this dependence explicit. [S5] [S6]

Research inference. Use progressively richer target approximations to initialize searches, then refine and select on the original target. This may widen the basin of attraction for the body shape. It can also steer away from the best full-detail solution; keep some direct full-target searches as a control.

What to inspect. Sharp turns demand high curvature or special behavior such as a stationary point/cusp. Adding points to a target does not add degrees of freedom to a mechanism. Plot where residuals concentrate rather than assuming extra joints always help. The three targets are separate tasks with separate reference points; success on the round body is not evidence of success on the full silhouette.

Proposed experiment. Compare direct full-target initialization with a low-to-high harmonic schedule, using equal search effort and the unchanged final evaluator. Record losses at each stage, but never substitute a descriptor distance for the submission score.

Later · Building the mechanism

10. Tolerances, collisions, force transmission, and stock parts

A kinematically valid, low-error mechanism is not yet a robust physical machine.

Domain knowledge. Robust synthesis considers how dimensional variation changes performance. Da Lio models uncertain link lengths and optimizes expected path deviation. Only the publisher abstract was available here, so no detailed implementation claim is made. [S12] Transmission angle adds a separate force-transfer consideration. [S3]

Project connection. The local objectives do not assess link thickness, pin clearances, out-of-plane assembly layers, collisions, torque, or dynamic loads. A 2D crossing may be physically realizable with layers, but layers create packaging and bearing requirements. These considerations should be tracked separately unless the task explicitly adopts them as constraints.

Practical extension. Perturb independent link dimensions and anchor locations using stated manufacturing tolerances, solve closure consistently, and inspect error spread and motion failures. Randomly perturbing every joint at every frame is not a physical tolerance model. Compare sensitivity over the complete cycle, especially near tangencies.

Reading direction. ReLink explores synthesis from a finite inventory of standard parts and a path–environmental-impact tradeoff. It is a July 2026 revision of a 2025 preprint. Its stock-part representation is relevant to fabrication; its objective is not the challenge's material metric. [S11]

04 / What to test next

A small sequence of discriminating experiments.

These are proposed tests, in dependency order. Each should answer one question before it is combined with the others.

00

Establish a trustworthy baseline.

Question: does the unmodified pipeline produce finite objectives and reproducible saved-file scores for known-valid inputs?

Record: code revision, seed, target, parameter settings, valid-candidate fraction, search evaluations, gradient calls, compilation time, warmed search time, and the evaluator's three score fields. Inspect curves and complete motion. Investigate invalid-value masking separately if finite outputs fail.

A proposed change cannot be credited with improvement if the baseline evaluation is not functioning.

01

Preserve and diversify valid initialization.

Compare: the existing starter initialization against a mixture of small perturbations of valid seeds and the supplied randomizer. Keep search operators and evaluation budget fixed.

Learn: whether early search is limited by invalid geometry or by lack of diversity. Count attempted generation work, not only successful candidates.

02

Make local refinement respect material.

Compare: the notebook's distance-only refinement against several material-budget-constrained runs, starting from the same candidates. Preserve the unrefined archive in both cases.

Learn: whether local accuracy gains also improve the official set-level objective. Reject non-finite or invalid steps; score the saved union using the existing evaluator.

03

Spend the same local-search budget on different starts.

Compare: best-distance elites against representatives spread across compatible geometry/topology groups and objective-space gaps.

Learn: whether repeated refinement falls into the same basin. Use equal numbers of starts and a matched refinement budget. Only then test contribution-based archive pruning if capacity becomes relevant.

04

Change one representation or target schedule.

Compare separately: ordered-parent topology variation versus raw edge-bit variation; or a harmonic continuation schedule versus direct original-target search.

Learn: whether a domain-informed representation improves official results at comparable cost. Neither experiment needs a new general-purpose optimizer framework.

Minimum evidence for choosing a method: start with a bounded pilot, then repeat the promising comparisons over several fixed seeds (five is a reasonable initial choice, not a statistical guarantee). Report median and spread per target, failed-run counts, and the overall normalized score. Compare both candidate-evaluation budgets and wall time because gradient calls and compilation have different costs.

05 / Reading route

Read for a decision, not just a bibliography.

  1. Understand what can be simulated.

    Read LinkEdit §§3–4 beside _kin.py and _JAX.py. Be able to explain a two-parent reconstruction, its two branches, and failure at tangency. Use the circle explorer to check your intuition. S1

  2. Understand what earns credit.

    Read the local evaluator and geometry utilities, then NSGA-II's selection description and SMS-EMOA §2. Be able to explain why an archive of tradeoffs can outperform its best-distance member. S7 · S8 · L1–L3

  3. Learn how to choose refinement starts.

    Read Sancibrian's hybrid-selection methods and §6 conclusions. Separate accuracy, runtime, and robustness; decide how you would translate the experiment to two objectives. S9

  4. Learn where shape descriptors can mislead.

    Read Sharma's parameterization argument, then Qian's timing and order-defect construction. Use the real-target explorer; propose a screening or continuation method while preserving original-scale evaluation. S5 · S6

  5. Read wider only when a limitation is measured.

    Use Pan for joint topology/geometry formulations, Gonzalez Rojas for constraint-aware dimensional synthesis, and ReLink/Da Lio for buildability. These are background and alternatives, not demonstrated CP1 upgrades. S4 · S10 · S11 · S12

Scope, evidence limits, and what was deliberately deferred

This is a focused research synthesis, not an exhaustive systematic review. It combines classic kinematics and multiobjective optimization with selected 2024–2026 work and current official software documentation. Source publication dates are distinguished from access dates and preprint revisions. Several papers already present in the project were used directly; no claim is made to have newly discovered them.

No existing notebook, solver, paper, or submission was edited. No official score was computed; existing Python lacked JAX/pymoo, and research did not require installing an optimizer environment. Browser demonstrations are educational calculations only. Trained models, a new solver, a new optimization framework, and fabrication analysis were not implemented. Local source/PDF links require this HTML to remain in the project's gpt folder; the report text and explorers are self-contained.
